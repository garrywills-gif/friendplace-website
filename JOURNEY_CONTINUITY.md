# FriendPlace Journey Continuity

_Written by Garry, July 2026. This principle sits above the
per-surface principles files (`/app/website/PUBLIC_EXPERIENCE_PRINCIPLES.md`,
future mobile and admin equivalents). Anything that spans two or more
surfaces answers to this file first._

---

## The principle

We are not building a marketing website with a chatbot attached.

We are building **the first chapter of the FriendPlace story**.

The website, George/Georgia, the app and Mission Control should all
feel like parts of the same world. Someone should be able to move
from:

- The website
- Registering Their Interest
- The welcome email
- Downloading the app
- First login
- Everyday use

...without ever feeling they've changed products or met a different
companion.

It should feel like **one continuous journey**.

## Why this matters

Continuity is not aesthetic polish. It is trust.

The moment someone feels the tone shift, the voice shift, the visual
language shift, or the companion suddenly behave differently, we've
broken the illusion that FriendPlace is a place — and reduced it to a
collection of screens. Loneliness is broken by places, not by
software.

Every seam we let show is a small betrayal of the welcome we promised.

## What this asks of every design and engineering decision

- **One companion. One voice. One tone.** George on the website is
  the same George on the app and in the welcome email. If a phrase
  wouldn't sound right from the same mouth in all four places, the
  phrase is wrong.
- **One visual language.** Same palette. Same butterfly. Same type.
  Same motion curves. Same rhythm of white space. A screen from the
  website next to a screen from the app should feel like siblings,
  not distant cousins.
- **No product handoffs.** No "you're now leaving FriendPlace" moments.
  No "click here to open the app" that feels like a URL change. Every
  transition is a soft continuation.
- **Companion memory across surfaces.** The George/Georgia the visitor
  chose on the website is the same one who signs the welcome email,
  the same one who greets them on first login, the same one who says
  "welcome back" every day after.
- **Language stays consistent.** If it's "Have a chat" on the website,
  it's not "Send a message" in the app. If it's "Come in" on the
  landing, it's not "Sign up" at the RYI form.
- **Even Mission Control is part of this world.** The back office isn't
  a different product; it's the room behind the counter of the same
  community centre. Same warmth, same voice, same butterfly.

## Practical rules of thumb

Before merging anything that touches two or more surfaces, ask:

1. **Same voice?** Would George/Georgia say this the same way in every
   place it appears?
2. **Same visual grammar?** Colours, type, spacing, motion — all from
   the shared token set?
3. **Same companion identity?** If the visitor chose Georgia on the
   website, does Georgia (not George, not a generic "we") speak to
   them here?
4. **No jarring transition?** Does the moment before flow naturally
   into the moment after, or does it feel like changing channels?

If any answer is no, the seam is showing. Fix it before shipping.

## The moment this all pays off

When someone downloads FriendPlace, opens it for the first time, and
their chosen companion says **"Welcome back"** — and it feels true.

They aren't meeting an AI for the first time. They're reconnecting
with someone they already know. The website was chapter one. The app
is chapter two. They never left the same story.
